We study cross-resolution face recognition, where a low-resolution probe face is matched against high-resolution gallery faces. We compare two ways of closing the quality gap: degrading the gallery images, and super-resolving the probe images with face hallucination. Experiments on SCFace and DroneSURF show that hallucination only helps when probe quality is high enough, and that a hybrid of both works best for relatively good probes, all with off-the-shelf face recognition models that are not retrained on the target domain.
